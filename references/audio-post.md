# Voice, music, and sound post-production

## Audio plan

Treat dialogue, effects, ambience, and music as separate tracks in the production plan. For every cue, record its scene/shot ID, in/out intent, narrative purpose, source/provenance, and approval state. Do not promise synthetic voice, voice cloning, lip sync, music generation, stems, or mastering unless the active tool is confirmed to support it and the user has the rights/consent needed.

## Voice direction

Create a voice brief per character: vocal age range, energy, pace, diction, emotional baseline, scene intention, and accessibility/language needs. Describe performance qualities rather than naming or copying a real performer. Mark dialogue as `final`, `scratch`, or `guide` so editorial choices do not silently become final audio.

For a dialogue line, include: speaker, exact approved text, intention, action/beat, anticipated duration only if measured in the active environment, and response/overlap rule. Avoid claiming word-perfect timing from a written script.

## Music and SFX

Use music to support a documented turn: setup, rising tension, reveal, emotional release, or transition. Specify energy, instrumentation families, tempo feel, and edit points without copying a protected composition or style-identifying a living artist.

For SFX, write the physical source and story function (`small paper map unfolds; confirms discovery`) rather than only a vague label. Maintain room tone/ambience continuity between cuts unless the edit deliberately changes environment.

## Mix and accessibility handoff

Before final export, verify dialogue intelligibility, intentional overlaps, abrupt ambience/music changes, captions/subtitles where requested, and rights/provenance notes. State the target loudness/delivery requirement only when the user supplies it or the delivery platform documentation is confirmed.
