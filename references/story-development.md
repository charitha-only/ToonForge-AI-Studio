# Story, script, and storyboard development

## From premise to shootable plan

Start with a premise, audience, format, and change the protagonist experiences. Write the logline before a scene list: it should identify the protagonist, goal, obstacle, and consequence. Then make a beat sheet that assigns every beat a visible change. A beat that cannot be seen, heard, or edited is not yet a production instruction.

Convert approved beats into a script only to the depth requested. Keep dialogue playable, give each line a speaking character and intended action, and avoid using narration to explain a visual action that could be shown. Record whether dialogue is final, temporary, or merely a direction for later voice work.

## Storyboard procedure

For every storyboard panel, record its scene/shot ID, framing, character positions, action, dialogue/audio cue, and start/end state. Storyboards are planning artifacts: do not say a panel or animatic was generated unless a real tool created it.

Use this coverage order when it serves the scene:

1. Establish geography and the dramatic question.
2. Show the action change in a readable master or medium shot.
3. Add close-ups, inserts, or reaction shots only when they clarify emotion, information, rhythm, or continuity.
4. End on a state that gives the next shot a stable starting point.

## Prompt-to-panel rule

One promptable shot should express one dominant visual event. Put dialogue in the audio cue sheet rather than expecting image/video generation to render readable text. When a tool does not support a storyboard, provide a text panel plan with the shot ledger instead.

## Story checks

- The protagonist has a specific desire, obstacle, decision, and consequence.
- Every scene changes knowledge, emotion, location, relationship, or objective.
- The visual plan establishes who is where, doing what, and why before cutting tightly.
- Comedy, action, or emotion has enough setup for its payoff.
- The ending resolves the episode question or intentionally creates a documented serial hook.
